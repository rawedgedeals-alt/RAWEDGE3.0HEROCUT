What a wagering requirement actually is

A wagering (playthrough) requirement is the total amount of eligible bets a promotion demands before bonus money — and usually any winnings from it — can be withdrawn. It is not a fee and it is not a single bet: it is cumulative stakes, added up bet after bet. Until the counter reaches the total, the cashier stays locked no matter what your balance says.

Why the base changes everything

"30×" is only half a sentence. 30× the bonus and 30× the deposit plus bonus are different prices wearing the same headline: on a $100 deposit with a $100 bonus, the first is $3,000 of betting and the second is $6,000. Operators are not obliged to make the base obvious. Our walkthrough of how to read casino bonus terms shows where the exact calculation hides in a real terms page.

Why game contribution is the quiet multiplier

Not every bet counts in full. Terms carry a contribution table: slots commonly count 100% of each bet toward the requirement, while table games, live casino and some other categories often count far less — and a few count zero. Contribution stretches the requirement by simple division. A $3,000 requirement at 100% contribution takes $3,000 of bets. Play only games that count 10%, and the same bonus takes $30,000 of bets to clear. Players who skip the contribution table are doing the casino's favorite kind of math: none.

Worked example

Deposit $100, take a $100 bonus, wagering 30× on the bonus only, playing slots at 100% contribution: $100 × 30 = $3,000 in total bets before anything can be withdrawn. At a $2 average bet, that is roughly 1,500 bets. Switch the base to deposit + bonus and the same offer becomes $6,000 — about 3,000 bets. Nothing about the headline changed. Only the fine print did.

Before you deposit

  • Find the wagering base in the terms — bonus only, or deposit + bonus?
  • Find the contribution rate for the games you actually play.
  • Check the deadline, the maximum bet while wagering, and any maximum cashout on bonus winnings.
  • Run the total here and ask honestly whether you would place that much play anyway, bonus or no bonus.

If the answer is no, the bonus is not a discount — it is a commitment you are buying. Clearing a requirement also never guarantees a remaining balance or a profit; it only unlocks the door.
